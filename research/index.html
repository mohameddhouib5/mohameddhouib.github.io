    Master’s thesis

    Predicting adaptation quality for low-resource languages when reusing pretrained speech-synthesis models under data and compute constraints, with application to Tunisian Arabic.

    Thesis grade: 17/20. The work studied how to select and adapt pretrained text-to-speech systems before expensive fine-tuning, with the goal of improving model reuse for languages with limited speech resources.

    Research methodology

    1. Fine-tuned six pretrained TTS architectures on a three-hour Tunisian Arabic corpus.
    2. Evaluated five data proportions with a uniform 12-hour compute budget, covering 30 configurations and 318 checkpoints.
    3. Built an automatic evaluation pipeline for synthesized speech and trained a predictor of adaptation quality.
    4. Validated the predictions through a listening campaign with native Tunisian Arabic speakers.

    Datasets and evaluation

    • Language: Tunisian Arabic, a low-resource language.
    • Speech data: approximately three hours for adaptation experiments.
    • Evaluation scale: nearly 98,000 synthesized utterances.
    • Metrics: character error rate (CER), predicted mean opinion score (MOS), and speaker similarity.
    • Human validation: 1,163 judgments from 38 native Tunisian Arabic listeners.
